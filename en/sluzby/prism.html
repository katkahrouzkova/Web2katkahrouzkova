WHEN PRISM MAKES SENSE AND WHAT IT UNCOVERS

"I enjoy my work, but it completely drains me."

Natural working style

Understanding how you operate when you are free to be yourself — what recharges you, what drains you over time, and where you are already working against your own grain.

"There are some people I just can't seem to get on the same page with."

Working with others

Better understanding of differences in communication, decision-making, and working pace — and how to work with them in day-to-day collaboration.

"When I'm under stress, I react in ways I later regret."

Functioning under pressure

Recognising how you respond under stress, change, or high demands — and how to build healthier patterns of response.

"Am I even suited for a management role?"

Adapting to a role

Understanding where you naturally draw on your strengths and where you are adapting to a role or to the expectations of others.

Three perspectives on how you operate

  • Natural style — how you operate when you can be yourself. What gives you energy and what comes naturally.
  • Adapted style — how you adjust to your environment, the expectations of others, or your work role.
  • Pressure and stress — how you respond under stress, time pressure, or in demanding situations.

It is often the differences between these three perspectives that reveal where tension, fatigue, or misunderstanding originates.

Stories from practice

You may recognise yourself in these

"As a CEO I need to be able to handle conflict. But I naturally avoid it."

— client transitioning into a CEO role

A client moving from head of research into a CEO role. His team trusts him — but when something goes wrong in the team, instead of addressing it directly, he waits to see if things will somehow settle down. In the meantime, frustration grows.

What the PRISM Benchmark showed

  • •Strong orientation toward people, relationships, and harmony.
  • •Avoidance of confrontation and raising difficult topics.
  • •An unusual combination: high drive and ambition paired with a diplomatic style.

Where the work is heading

  • •Raising difficult topics earlier, before they escalate.
  • •Being more decisive in unpopular situations.
  • •Expanding how he uses his natural style — not changing it.

Leadership at CEO level can look very different from person to person. Drive and the ability to lead people do not come in just one style.

"All my life I felt I should be more thorough and better with details."

— client looking for a new career direction

After years in finance roles abroad he was returning to the Czech Republic and looking for what to do next. He had the sense that things others found easy cost him significantly more effort. Details often escaped him; precise, methodical work left him exhausted.

What PRISM showed

  • •Strong abilities in leadership, innovation, and finding new paths.
  • •Ability to get people moving, persuade, and find solutions where no one else sees them.
  • •Detail, routine, and precise execution cost him significantly more energy.

What that meant

  • •He had spent years building a career in an environment that worked against his natural style.
  • •It was not that he was less capable — he had been fighting himself.
  • •He left with a clearer picture of where his way of operating would be an advantage, not an obstacle.

Many of us choose our careers early and then spend years trying to improve at things that do not come naturally — instead of building more on where we can be at our strongest.
