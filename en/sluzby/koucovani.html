How I work in coaching

  • I do not provide life instructions or ready-made solutions. I believe that decisions people reach on their own are the ones that actually last.
  • I ask questions that are not always comfortable. That is often exactly where the most important insights and new possibilities emerge.
  • I combine a coaching approach with experience from the corporate world. I understand the topics of leadership, career transitions, people development, and team dynamics.
  • Practicality matters to me. An interesting insight is not enough — we also look for how to translate it into everyday life and work.

EXAMPLES FROM PRACTICE

Topics I work on with clients

"I can't be their manager, their friend, and their mum all at once."

— client in a managerial role

In the first session we opened the topic of how to assert her views within the team. It quickly became clear that the issue was not a lack of managerial skills. The topic was deeper — she had a strong inner need for people to be constantly satisfied with her and to like her.

How it showed up

  • •Inner tension at the mere thought of disappointing someone.
  • •Avoiding difficult conversations and giving feedback.
  • •Making compromises to preserve good relationships, often at the cost of her own exhaustion.

What happened during coaching

We stopped working on management techniques and focused on her personal boundaries. We examined her behavioural patterns and separated her personal identity from her managerial role. In the safe space of coaching we explored how to communicate expectations firmly without losing her humanity.

Outcome and impact in practice

  • •She found inner calm and learned to tolerate temporary "unpopularity".
  • •She began communicating her boundaries clearly, without guilt or apology.
  • •She stepped out of the rescuer role — and paradoxically gained far greater professional respect from her team, and felt relieved.

The greatest challenge in leadership is sometimes not leading people at all, but the ability to hold your own boundaries and resist the urge to please everyone.

"I am 24 and I am afraid I will burn out."

— client two years after graduation

He came with the feeling that the problem was the volume of work and that he would not be able to sustain the pace he was living at. During the conversations it became clear that it was not just about the number of tasks, but also about which type of work naturally suited him and which did not. Some activities absorbed him for hours; others he had to force himself through.

What happened during coaching

  • •He began distinguishing which tasks give him energy and which tend to drain it.
  • •He noticed that with certain activities he switches into a "I need to try harder" mode, even when he is already feeling long-term fatigue.
  • •He realised that work did not end for him when he left the office.

Outcome and impact in practice

  • •He introduced small rituals that help him focus better and gradually separate work time from personal time.
  • •He brought exercise and more time for himself back into his life.
  • •He learned to consciously "close" the working day and switch his mind to other areas of life.

Not every fatigue means you are doing too much. Sometimes it is a signal that you have been operating for too long in a way that is not aligned with how you naturally work.
